LeptoCOS: core outcome set for leptomeningeal disease

Long list of candidate outcomes from a systematic review of 46 trials (1982 to 2025) · COMET Initiative Study 3224

LeptoCOS is a multidisciplinary, international initiative developing a core outcome set for leptomeningeal disease (LMD): an agreed minimum set of outcomes every trial should measure and report. This site presents the long list of candidate outcomes from the systematic review, the evidence behind it, and how to take part.

Registered as COMET Initiative Study 3224 · informed by the COMET Handbook and the COS-STAD standards; the systematic review follows PRISMA reporting guidance.

How LeptoCOS sub-domains relate to the COMET taxonomy. The COMET taxonomy is a standardised, high-level framework that applies across all areas of health research. To capture distinctions that matter in leptomeningeal disease, such as leptomeningeal, intracranial and extracranial disease control, standardised outcomes were first grouped into more granular, disease-specific sub-domains, and each sub-domain was then mapped onto the COMET taxonomy and its five core areas. This sub-domain layer is an addition to the taxonomy rather than a substitute for it; it retains disease-specific detail while keeping the set comparable with the wider core outcome set literature.

Long list · definitions

Definitions of the 56 long-list outcomes

The 56 candidate outcomes proposed for rating in the eDelphi, each with a plain-language definition, its sub-domain and its COMET core area. Filter, search or sort.

How often an outcome was reported is not evidence of its importance; the eDelphi will ask how important each outcome is, not how common it is.

How to read these definitions

The study's defined start event means the origin each trial used, for example leptomeningeal diagnosis, treatment start, randomisation or enrolment. Some trials do not state it.

Extracranial means systemic disease outside the central nervous system. Spinal leptomeningeal disease is recorded as leptomeningeal, not extracranial.

Time to progression (TTP) counts progression rather than death in its standard definition. Each trial's stated death handling applies, and unspecified censoring rules are not inferred.

In these definitions, clinical benefit rate (CBR) requires stable disease to last a minimum stated duration; disease control rate (DCR) has no separate stable disease duration requirement. The literature sometimes uses these terms interchangeably, and ordinary response confirmation does not by itself change DCR to CBR.

Background

Why leptomeningeal disease needs a core outcome set

Leptomeningeal disease is a late-stage complication of cancer. It carries a poor prognosis and is increasingly recognised. Trials are small and scattered over more than four decades. When they do not share an outcome language, their results cannot readily be pooled, compared or graded for guidelines. A core outcome set is the agreed minimum set of outcomes every trial should measure and report, not a limit on what else may be reported.

Fragmented reporting

368 outcomes reduce to 56 standardised outcomes; only 3 are reported by at least half of the trials.

Priority often unstated

142 of 368 outcomes (39%) carry no recorded or assigned priority.

Few patient or resource outcomes

Quality of life reported by 9 of 46 trials; resource use by 1 of 46.

Finding 1 · inconsistent definitions

Leptomeningeal treatment response is defined inconsistently

Even the most-reported response outcome is defined in many different ways.

Reported by 25 of 46 trials; only 7 used a RANO-LM-based standard (6 RANO-LM, 1 modified); the other 18 did not: 16 used study-specific criteria, 1 used EANO-ESMO response assessment criteria and 1 used RECIST v1.1 with the leptomeningeal lesion as a non-target lesion.

N = number of trials using that definition; definitions without a number were each used by one trial.

RECIST 1.1 (LM as non-measurable disease)Leptomeningeal disease is listed under RECIST 1.1 as “truly non-measurable” disease. Reporting rigour

Outcome priority as reported

Priority (primary, secondary or exploratory) was recorded as each trial stated it. Where a trial gave no explicit label, an outcome was assigned primary only if it informed the sample-size calculation or was specified as an outcome objective; a general statement of purpose was not enough, and secondary or exploratory status was never assigned. Priority was not used to include or exclude outcomes. More than a third of outcomes (39%) carry no recorded or assigned priority.

Transparency

Methods and limitations

This work is informed by the COMET Handbook and COS-STAD; the systematic review is reported according to PRISMA. Outcomes were extracted from published leptomeningeal disease trials, grouped into 56 standardised outcomes and mapped to 20 LeptoCOS sub-domains and the five COMET core areas. This is a descriptive catalogue of reported outcomes, not a core outcome set.
